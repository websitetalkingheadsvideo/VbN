# Eddy Valiant
**Clan:** Malkavian **Generation:** 12th **Sect:** Camarilla (loosely aligned)  
**Concept:** Hard-boiled private investigator in a city that forgot the truth  
**Nature:** Cynic **Demeanor:** Gallant  

---

## Biography

Eddy Valiant was once the kind of detective you only read about in dime-store novels—good instincts, bad habits, and a moral compass that spun wildly between whiskey bottles.  
In the late seventies, he made his name solving impossible cases in the darker corners of Phoenix: missing persons, blackmail, things the police didn’t want to touch.  
Then one night in 1981, a case led him to a killer who wasn’t human.  
He caught the monster; the monster caught him back.

His Embrace came like a punchline he didn’t see coming.  
The sire—long vanished—left him with fragmented memories and a mind that started narrating itself in third person.  
Eddy became the protagonist of his own noir, condemned to hear life described in voice-over.  
Most Kindred call it madness; he calls it “good writing.”

After decades in the shadows, Eddy’s still on the case.  
He operates out of a cramped office above a pawn shop on 7th and Van Buren—*Valiant Detective Agency*.  
His ghoul secretary, Marilyn Chase, keeps the mortal world spinning by day while Eddy hunts secrets by night.  
He’s the Camarilla’s unofficial bloodhound, called in when politics require deniable curiosity.  
He doesn’t trust them, and they return the favor.

To the Anarchs, he’s an oddity with a badge; to the Harpy, he’s comic relief.  
But when the Prince of Phoenix is murdered—the same night a fledgling is introduced to court—Eddy smells the rot beneath the velvet.  
His intuition cuts through lies like cigarette smoke through sunlight.  
In a city full of masks, he’s the one creature too jaded to wear one convincingly.

---

## Psychological Profile

Eddy’s Malkavian curse manifests as **Cinematic Delusion**—he experiences reality as an unending detective film.  
He occasionally speaks his inner monologue aloud, unaware he’s doing it.  
This quirk disarms others; his insight, however, is razor-sharp.  
Behind the sarcasm is a Kindred who still believes in justice, even if he doubts redemption.

He maintains Humanity 6 — clinging to mortal ideals like an anchor chain in rising tide.  
He feeds sparingly, preferring bagged blood or volunteer donors who come to him seeking “closure.”  
His investigation files read like confessions; he’s solving the city’s sins one case at a time.

---

## Relationships & Influence

- **Marilyn Chase (ghoul):** secretary, caretaker, and unspoken conscience.  
- **Sarah Hansen (Toreador):** frequent client and confidante; their rapport borders on friendship, tempered by mutual cynicism.  
- **Kerry (Gangrel):** occasional tracker partner—one of the few Eddy trusts to watch his back outside city limits.  
- **Cordelia Fairchild (Harpy):** sees him as useful but untidy; he calls her “the velvet executioner.”  
- **Seneschal Roland:** respects Eddy’s results, despises his attitude.  

Eddy owes small boons across every faction; he collects favors like others collect scars.  
His reputation: dependable, discreet, and impossible to intimidate.

---

## Current Status (1994)

When the Prince dies at Hawthorn Estate, Eddy’s already on site—just another shadow slipping in through a side door.  
Some say he was hired to vet security; others think he was following a lead that got him too close to the truth.  
Either way, he becomes one of the first on the scene when the Camarilla’s order unravels.  
Now, Phoenix’s factions are calling on the one detective who might actually find the killer—or the scapegoat.

## Storyteller Notes

- **Role:** truth-seeker, moral foil, reluctant mentor figure.  
- **Theme:** *Perception vs. Reality* — the detective who sees everything except a future.  
- **Disciplines:** Auspex 3, Obfuscate 2, Dementation 2.  
- **Archetype fit:** Classic Malkavian noir archetype — a mind fractured into insight.  
- **Visual aesthetic:** smoky amber lighting, cool teal shadows, 1940s silhouette in 1990s grit.  
- **Cinematic tone:** Harsh contrast, cigarette smoke, and slow jazz underscore; every entrance feels like the start of another case.
